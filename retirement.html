Retirement

A New Trail Begins

Retirement is not the end of a sled dog’s story. It is a thoughtful transition into a pace and lifestyle suited to the individual dog.

A New Pace

Care continues when competition ends.

Retirement should reflect the whole dog—not simply its age or racing history.

Some dogs remain active and enjoy recreational running, hiking, or familiar routines. Others prefer quieter days, shorter outings, more rest, or a home environment built around companionship. The right next chapter depends on health, temperament, enthusiasm, recovery, comfort, and individual preference.

Life After Competition

A different rhythm can still be a full life.

Recreational Activity

Some retired dogs continue to enjoy light running, hiking, walks, or other activities adapted to their health and energy.

Companionship

Time with familiar people and dogs can remain central to daily life, especially for dogs who value social routines.

Comfort & Recovery

Older dogs may benefit from softer resting spaces, shorter outings, easier access, more frequent monitoring, and adjusted care.

Enrichment & Play

Retirement does not mean inactivity. Curiosity, play, exploration, training games, and affection can remain important throughout life.

Movement at a New Pace

Retirement may still include walks, easy hikes, exploration, or recreational activity. The pace changes to match the dog—not the other way around.

Lifetime Responsibility

The relationship does not end when the race does.

Responsible care continues through aging, changing needs, quiet companionship, and every stage that follows competition.

CARE STANDARD Retirement is part of the journey, and planning for it is part of responsible care.
