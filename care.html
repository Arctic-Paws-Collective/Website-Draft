Canine Care

Exceptional Performance Begins With Exceptional Care

Every mile on the trail is supported by thousands of thoughtful decisions made long before the race begins. Nutrition, conditioning, veterinary care, recovery, and the bond between musher and dog all work together to support healthy, enthusiastic athletes.

Explore Canine Care →

01 · Nutrition

Fuel for endurance.

Working sled dogs require carefully balanced nutrition to support strength, stamina, recovery, and resilience in demanding conditions.

Meals can be adjusted for training intensity, temperature, age, body condition, hydration needs, and each dog’s individual response. Nutrition is not treated as a fixed formula; it changes as the dog’s work and environment change.

CARE STANDARD Nutrition is tailored to the individual dog—not a one-size-fits-all feeding program.

02 · Veterinary Care

Health monitored year-round.

Preventive care includes far more than race-day examinations. Routine observation, physical assessment, paw protection, hydration monitoring, and recovery checks help identify small concerns before they become larger problems.

Routine Focus

  • Preventive physical examinations
  • Vaccination and parasite-control planning
  • Paw, pad, nail, and bootie care
  • Hydration and body-condition monitoring
  • Muscle soreness and recovery assessment
  • Dental and whole-body health
CARE STANDARD Preventive care is always preferable to reactive care.

03 · Conditioning & Recovery

Strength through preparation.

Conditioning develops endurance gradually while preserving enthusiasm, confidence, teamwork, and sound movement.

Workload is balanced with recovery. Rest, sleep, hydration, massage, feeding, and quiet observation are all part of training—not interruptions to it.

CARE STANDARD Rest is part of training.

04 · Life Beyond Racing

They are athletes—and individuals.

Most of a sled dog’s life happens away from competition. Dogs play, rest, explore, form friendships, learn routines, seek affection, and develop distinct preferences and personalities.

Family First

Daily care is built around familiarity, observation, handling, and the relationship between dogs and the people who know them best.

Individual Needs

Age, temperament, appetite, confidence, energy, and recovery vary from dog to dog. Care should reflect those differences.

Joy Matters

Enthusiasm, engagement, and willingness are meaningful parts of assessing how a dog is responding to work and daily life.

05 · Retirement

Another chapter, not an ending.

Retirement is part of responsible long-term planning.

Retirement plans vary. Many dogs remain with their mushers, continue lighter activity, or move to carefully selected homes suited to their individual needs.

CARE STANDARD Retirement is part of the journey.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about sled dog care.

Do sled dogs enjoy running?

Many purpose-bred sled dogs show strong enthusiasm for running, pulling, and working with a team. Individual willingness and behavior should still be observed closely.

What do sled dogs eat?

Diets vary by workload, climate, age, body condition, and individual response. High-energy work may require adjustments in calories, fat, protein, hydration, and feeding frequency.

How are injuries prevented?

Progressive conditioning, routine examination, paw care, proper equipment, close observation, rest, and recovery planning all contribute to prevention.

How often do they receive veterinary care?

Care is not limited to emergencies. Preventive examinations, vaccination planning, ongoing monitoring, and race or event checks can all be part of a broader health program.

What happens when a sled dog retires?

Retirement plans vary. Many dogs remain with their mushers, continue lighter activity, or move to carefully selected homes suited to their individual needs.
